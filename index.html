What we train

Four things, taught properly.

Each one stands on its own, and each one makes the others better. Most students start with taekwondo and pick up a second later.

Taekwondo

The Korean art the academy is built on — kicking, footwork, forms and controlled sparring, graded belt by belt.

  • Poomsae (forms) and basics
  • Kicking drills and pad work
  • Sparring under supervision

Self defence

What to do when there is no referee. Distance, awareness, breaking a grip and getting out — practised slowly first, then at speed.

  • Escapes and releases
  • Awareness and distance
  • Suitable for beginners

Fitness

Conditioning built around what a martial artist actually needs: hips that open, legs that last, a core that holds a stance.

  • Mobility and flexibility
  • Strength and endurance
  • Train it on its own or alongside

Swimming

The academy's second sport. Low-impact conditioning and a life skill every child in the family should have.

  • Beginners welcome
  • Water confidence first
  • Ask about the current season

Why SAFE

Everyone gets to be the athlete in the room.

Sports and Fitness Enthusiasts Academy takes beginners — the shy eight-year-old, the office worker who hasn't trained since school, the parent who signed up after watching their kid's grading. Nobody starts good at this. The floor is built for people on day one.

Progress here is visible and it is earned. You learn a form, you drill it until it stops being a thought, you test for the next belt. Children who train long enough end up in the academy's demonstration team, performing at G-8 Markaz in front of their families.

    Before you come in

    Questions parents actually ask.

    Children make up a large part of the academy — the kids' classes run their own demonstrations and gradings. The right starting age depends on the child, so message the academy with your child's age and they'll tell you which group to join.

    Fees are confirmed directly by the academy. Ask on WhatsApp and you'll get the current figure for the discipline and age group you're asking about.

    Not on your first day. Loose clothes you can kick in and bare feet are enough. The academy will tell you when to get a dobok and where.

    Everyone on the floor started as a white belt. Beginners are grouped by grade, not thrown in with senior students, so your first weeks are spent on stance and basics with people at the same stage.

    Sparring is part of taekwondo, but it is introduced in stages and always supervised — light contact, matched partners, protective gear. Nobody spars before their instructor says they're ready.
